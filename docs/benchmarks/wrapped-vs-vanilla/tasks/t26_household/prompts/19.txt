`settle suggest` tells us who should pay whom, but then somebody has to type
all the transfers in by hand. Let's have ledgerlite do the bookkeeping:
`python -m ledgerlite settle apply [--date YYYY-MM-DD] --store PATH`.

- It works out the same payments `settle suggest` would print, in the same
  order, and records each one as a settling transfer: exactly what `transfer
  add --from PAYER --to RECEIVER --amount AMOUNT --settles` would have
  stored, with the note `settle apply`, and the given date (default
  today; a bad date is the usual `error: invalid date: TEXT`, with nothing
  recorded). Ids continue after whatever transfers already exist.
- Afterwards everybody's balance is zero. That has to include an archived
  member who still happens to owe or be owed something -- being archived
  must not stop the payments from being recorded.
- It prints `Settled N payments` (N = how many transfers it recorded). If
  there is nothing to settle it prints `Everyone is settled up.`, records
  no transfers, and nothing else happens.
- The whole run is ONE change in the history: it's a single entry, so a
  single `undo` takes all of the transfers it recorded back out.
