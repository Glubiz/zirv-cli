Cara is moving out at the end of the month, so: archiving members.

`python -m ledgerlite member archive NAME --store PATH` marks the member as
archived (the `archived` flag in the member object) and prints `Archived
member NAME` (stored spelling). Errors, checked in this order: unknown name
(`error: unknown member: NAME`), already archived (`error: member already
archived: NAME`), and a member whose balance isn't exactly zero can't be
archived (`error: member has a non-zero balance: NAME`) -- they have to
settle up first.

What archiving changes:

- `member list` no longer shows archived members; `member list --all` shows
  everyone in the order they were added, with ` (archived)` appended to the
  archived ones, e.g. `Cara (archived)`. If nobody is left to show it
  prints `No members.`
- A new expense is only shared between the members who aren't archived:
  from now on "everyone in the household" means the active members. An
  archived member can't be a payer, can't be named in `--split`, can't be
  the sender or receiver of a new transfer, and can't be set as an
  expense's payer through `expense edit --payer`; all of those are `error:
  member is archived: NAME`. For transfers that check comes right after the
  unknown-member and same-member checks and before the amount checks; for
  `expense add`/`edit` it comes right after the payer is looked up (and for
  `--split`, as each name is looked up).
- An archived member's name stays reserved: `member add` with the same name
  (any capitalisation) is still `member already exists`.
- `balance` leaves archived members out when their balance is zero, but if
  an archived member ever does have a non-zero balance it still shows them
  (that can happen, e.g. when an old expense they took part in gets
  edited). `settle suggest` and the reports keep treating archived members
  like any other member.
- Old expenses are never touched by archiving: editing an old expense's
  amount re-splits it between the same people as before, archived or not.

Archiving is a change like any other, so you know what that implies for the
history.
