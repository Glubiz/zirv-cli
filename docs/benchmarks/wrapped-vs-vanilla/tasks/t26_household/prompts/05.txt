Good. Now the point of all this: nobody wants to work out by hand who
should pay whom. Add `python -m ledgerlite settle suggest --store PATH`,
which looks at the balances `balance` reports and prints a short list of
payments that would bring everybody back to zero. It must not change the
store.

Use this greedy procedure (it keeps the number of payments low, and I want
the output to be deterministic):

1. Among the members who owe money (negative balance), take the one who
   owes the most; among the members who are owed money (positive
   balance), take the one who is owed the most. Ties in either group go
   to whoever was added as a member first.
2. The first pays the second whatever is smaller of "what the debtor
   still owes" and "what the creditor is still owed". Print it as `PAYER
   pays RECEIVER AMOUNT` (two decimals), e.g. `Ben pays Ana 10.00`.
3. Reduce both balances by that amount, drop anyone who reaches zero, and
   repeat until nobody is left.

Payments are printed in the order they were generated, one per line. If
nobody owes anything, print `Everyone is settled up.`

Put the logic in `household.py` (not the CLI handler), working off the
same balance numbers `balance` uses.
