I really want an undo button for the operations that can dump a pile of
wrong transactions into my ledger in one shot: `import`, `import-ofx`, and
`recurring apply`. (Not `budget`/`reconcile` -- those are easy to fix by
hand, and not worth the complexity right now.)

Add `python -m ledgerlite undo --store PATH`: undoes the most recent of
those three operations, restoring the ledger's transactions to exactly
what they were right before that operation ran, and prints `Undid: <what
you're undoing>` -- for `import`, that's `import <csv_path>`; for
`import-ofx`, `import-ofx <ofx_path>`; for `recurring apply`, `recurring
apply <from>..<to>` (e.g. `recurring apply 2024-01..2024-03`). If there's
nothing to undo, print `Nothing to undo.` and exit 0 (not an error).

Also add `python -m ledgerlite undo --list --store PATH`, printing one
line per undo-able operation still on the stack, MOST RECENT FIRST, using
the exact same label text `undo` itself would print after "Undid: ". Same
`Nothing to undo.` when the stack is empty.

Undo only goes one operation at a time and only one level of history per
call (calling `undo` twice undoes the two most recent operations, in
order) -- there's no redo.
